But Ilúvatar had knowledge about Men, who [were] set among [the] chaos of [the] world, would stray [a] great many times and will not employ their gifts by harmony; and he said. “These also in their time will find that all that they do adds to the end only to the glory of my work.” Still the Elves believe that Men caused great sorrow to Manwe on many occasions, who has the greatest knowledge of [the] mind [of] Ilúvatar; for it seems that to the Elves, that Men resemble Melkor beyond all the Ainur, but forever he feared and hated them, also those who served him.
But Ilúvatar knew that Men, being set amid the turmoils of the powers of the world, would stray often, and would not use their gifts in harmony; and he said, ‘These too in their time shall find that all that they do redounds at the end only to the glory of my work.’ Yet the Elves believe that Men are often a grief to Manwë, who knows most of the mind of Ilúvatar; for it seems to the Elves that Men resemble Melkor most of all the Ainur, although he has ever feared and hated them, even those that served him.

1.24
Gu ann e·leinas hen chîn in·Edain guiar, air lû thent vi amar, ah ú-'wennin na den, a gwannar rong navan in·Edhil ú-istar. Ach in·Edhil rethir dem methed in·oer, ah amminai ah annûr e·meleth dín añ geven ah amar bân, ah annírol lo in·în ledir. Ach in·Edhil ú-‘wannar den amar ‘wanna, air bi di ndengin egor belir vi faegol (ah i·ñgyrth dâd nim hin bessar din); ach íphathred ú-nítha e·vellas dín, air bi dhrauthar voen edrenn bae menig; a gwanniel covar na them Bannas vi Ndor-Rodyn, oman ndandolathar aen1 vi lû gammui. Ach ionnath in·Edain firir î, ah awarthar en·amar; eni gaeth hen estanner din i·Nethail egor in·Ethyl. Gurth en·ammarth dín, en·ann nan Iluvadar2, i lo in·în lúdar i·Mbelain eithro anírathar. Ach Mbelegûr achant e·vorchant dín thar den, a changant den ‘u fuin, ah ethonc rhû e maeras, a goe ed estel. Ach miñ gwanwas, idh·Rodyn ebenner an in·Edhil vi Ndor-Rodyn i in·Edain ‘oiannathar vi Linnas in·Aeny Dadui; lo Iladar ú-bathant man meitha enin Edhil ab vethed amar, a Belegûr ú-echir hen.
Together with this gift of freedom [the] children [of] Men live, only [a] short time [in] the world, and [are] not bound to it, and depart soon whither the Elves do not have knowledge. But the Elves remain until [the] end [of] the days, and more-single and deeper their love for the earth and the whole world, and more-sorrowful while the years lengthen. But the Elves did not die until [the] world dies, only if slain or they fade in grief (and these two seeming deaths affect them); but age does not reduce their strength, only if they tire out by the ten-thousand ages; and having departed they-gather to the halls of Mandos in Valinor, whence they might return in appropriate time. But the sons of the Men die indeed, and forsake [the] world; for this reason they call them the Guests or the Strangers. Death [is] their fate, the gift of Ilúvatar, which while the years pass the Powers also they will desire. But Melkor cast his shadow across it, and confounded it together with darkness, and brought forth evil out of good, and fear out of hope. But in the past, the Valar told the Elves in Valinor that the Men will join in the Second Music of the Ainur; while Ilúvatar did not reveal what he intends for the Elves after the end of [the] world, and Melkor did not discover it.
It is one with this gift of freedom that the children of Men dwell only a short space in the world alive, and are not bound to it, and depart soon whither the Elves know not. Whereas the Elves remain until the end of days, and their love of the Earth and all the world is more single and more poignant therefore, and as the years lengthen ever more sorrowful. For the Elves die not till the world dies, unless they are slain or waste in grief (and to both these seeming deaths they are subject); neither does age subdue their strength, unless one grow weary of ten thousand centuries; and dying they are gathered to the halls of Mandos in Valinor, whence they may in time return. But the sons of Men die indeed, and leave the world; wherefore they are called the Guests, or the Strangers. Death is their fate, the gift of Ilúvatar, which as Time wears even the Powers shall envy. But Melkor has cast his shadow upon it, and confounded it with darkness, and brought forth evil out of good, and fear out of hope. Yet of old the Valar declared to the Elves in Valinor that Men shall join in the Second Music of the Ainur; whereas Ilúvatar has not revealed what he purposes for the Elves after the World’s end, and Melkor has not discovered it.
