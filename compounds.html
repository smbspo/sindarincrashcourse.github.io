1.6a Reading Compound Names

Creating names through compounds involves more than joining two dictionary headwords. Earlier word forms, sound changes, the meaning of each element, and the time when a name was formed can all affect the result. Fiona Jallings’ consonant chart is a useful starting point. You can also explore Telpefindelë | Celephinnel’s Quettamorphosis.

The names below are my own creations unless identified as an attested comparison. Their quoted glosses record my intended meanings. The source notes distinguish Tolkien’s Sindarin vocabulary, older Gnomish or Noldorin material adopted for Neo-Sindarin, and uncertain reconstructions. A documented element does not by itself establish the whole name or every step of its proposed history.

Open My original proposed process to read my working chains. The six-pointed star (✶) marks an early form; it does not by itself tell you whether Tolkien supplied that form or a later writer reconstructed it. Eldamo uses a separate asterisk (*) for a reconstruction. See its notation guide and the source notes below.

1.6b Historical Forms and Modern Coinages

For example, I created the sword name “Iron Light” using ang for “iron” and calad for “light”.

Source status: My own compound using Sindarin elements. ang and calad are documented, but the combined chain below is my proposal. The exact vowel loss and cluster reduction need their own support; the general cluster entry does not uniquely determine this result.

My original proposed process: Angalad

Let's run it through the process: ✶Aŋga·kalata > Angalad, AN-ga-lad, | [aŋga·kalata] > [aŋga·kalat] > [aŋga·galad] > [aŋggalad] > [aŋgalad]

Here is an attested example: ✶Gondo·ndor > Gondor, GON-dor, | [gondo·ndor] > [gondndor] > [gondor]

Attested comparison: Eldamo records this development of Gondor, citing Tolkien’s explanation in PE17/28. An attested reduction in one name is useful evidence, but its conditions still matter when considering another name.

A later compound can join already developed Sindarin elements. A reconstructed ancient compound starts with earlier forms and must account for the environments that existed at each stage. These approaches can produce different histories. You do not need to force every modern coinage through the entire historical rule list.

An example I received from Elaran on The Tolkien Forum contrasts Ceiradan and the more archaic proposal Ciriadan, with the intended meaning “Ship-Man”, using cair “ship”. These are forum proposals here, not examples of Tolkien attesting those complete name histories. The supplied development of the independent word is:

KIRjā > kirja > kerja > keria > keri > keir > kair

In the usual later pattern, ai occurs in a final syllable and ei appears when that syllable becomes non-final. The original examples erain and cair illustrate final ai. There are exceptions, so “ai can only appear in a final syllable” is too absolute. Compare rule 03900 and the historical account of cair. An element compounded before those changes occurred need not follow the history of the independent word.

Names do not always require two elements. Tolkien’s remarks below concern differentiation when using the names of things. They should not be expanded into a universal ban on a one-word name or epithet. Thurin is an attested name Finduilas gave Túrin, providing a direct counterexample to that broader claim.

The mere names of things, such as “hill, river, tree”, and especially of unique things, as “sun”, were not used, at any rate without differentiation: [...] for that would imply some kind of total equation or identity. An Elf (or Man) would not be called Anar “Sun” even to depict great glory or radiant vigour. [...] But an Elf or Man could be called Anárion, Anardil [etc.] [PE21/86]

1.6.1 Weapon Names

  • Civaglar, ci-MAG-lar, “Blade of Glory”, cim “blade”, + aglar “glory, brilliance, splendour, radiance”

    Source status: Neo-Sindarin adoption of older vocabulary. The name is presented here as my own creation.

    cim comes from archaic Gnomish. Paul Strack adopts it for Neo-Sindarin and suggests a primitive form with mb. That reconstruction does not establish the simple m to v development in my chain, since historical mb follows a different path. aglar is Sindarin. The spelling of the name and its printed pronunciation also disagree at v/m; the original reading is retained below for review.

    My original proposed process

    Process: ✶kim·aklara > kimaklar > kimaglar > kivaglar.

  • Cumeleg, CU-me-leg, “Mighty Bow”, cû “bow”, + beleg “great, mighty; large, big”

    Source status: Sindarin elements; proposed historical compound. The name is presented here as my own creation.

    cû has a documented earlier form containing m, so bringing that consonant into a historical analysis has a basis. beleg is also Sindarin, although its root history varies in Tolkien’s notes. The complete chain is my reconstruction. The restoration of a stop after a nasal and the later treatment of mb need to be evaluated in that historical order, rather than applied to any modern pair of words.

    My original proposed process

    Process: ✶kūma·beleke > kūma·veleke > kūma·velek > kūma·veleg > kūmveleg > kūmbeleg > kumbeleg > kummeleg > kumeleg

  • Athaedor, a-THAE-dor “Healing Wood”, athae “*healing herb, healing”, + taur “forest, wood”

    Source status: Sindarin elements; meaning and formation need qualification. The name is presented here as my own creation.

    athae is a Sindarin element, but its exact sense and whether it is a noun, an adjective, or both are inferred. Tolkien’s later account treats it as a Quenya borrowing. taur refers to a forest or woodland, so my intended “Wood” should not be read as a claim that this element means wood as a material. Diphthong reduction is variable: au to o is common, while ae to e is rare. The full inherited history proposed below is therefore not established simply by joining these elements.

    My original proposed process

    Process: ✶atʰajā·taurē > atʰaja·taure > aθaja·taure > aθaia·taure > aθaia·taur > aθaia·daur > aθaidaur > aθaedaur > aθaedor

  • Haldagnir, hal-DAG-nir, “Shadow Slayer”, N. hall¹ “veiled, hidden, shadowed, shady”, + dagnir “slayer”

    Source status: Noldorin vocabulary combined with Sindarin. The name is presented here as my own creation.

    hall¹ is a Noldorin adjective. Its adjectival meaning does not by itself establish the noun reading in my intended “Shadow Slayer”. dagnir is Sindarin and has a historical initial nasalized stop. The source history of the first element also needs to be distinguished from the starting form in my chain. Haldir offers a comparison for the first element, but does not prove every reduction in this longer cluster. Cluster simplification is a group of context-dependent changes, not permission to remove any middle consonant.

    My original proposed process

    Process: ✶skalla·ndak·ndēr > sxalla·ndak·ndēr > xalla·ndak·ndēr > xalla·ndak·ndīr > halla·ndak·ndīr > halla·ndag·ndīr > hallndagndīr > hallndagndīr > haldagnīr > haldagnir.

1.6.2 Character Names

  • Hennurin, HEN-nu-rin, “Secret Child”, hên “child”, + thurin “secret, hidden”

    Source status: Sindarin elements; the proposed sound history needs review. The name is presented here as my own creation.

    thurin is Sindarin, attested in Tolkien’s later writings. Its Ilkorin predecessor is thúren. The primitive reconstruction used in my chain belongs to the discussion of that Ilkorin form; it does not automatically explain Sindarin thurin. Eldamo explicitly notes uncertainty about the vowel history. hên is also Sindarin, but the vowel developments of the full compound need to be assessed in their own environment. Finally, internal nθ and n plus th at a recognized compound boundary need not behave alike. Compare boundary nasal loss and medial assimilation; the final step shown below is a proposal, not an automatic rule for every such compound.

    My original proposed process

    Process: ✶kʰinā·tʰūrinā > kʰina·tʰūrina > xina·θūrina > xena·θūrina > hena·θūrina > hena·θūrin > henθurin > hennurin

  • Mirwen, MIR-wen, “Jewel Maiden”, mîr “jewel, precious thing, treasure”, + gwend “maiden”

    Source status: Sindarin elements; an established name ending. The name is presented here as my own creation.

    mîr and gwend are Sindarin, and -wen is an established feminine name ending. However, Tolkien’s discussion suggests that names used a form without the final dental consonant even in older spelling. The complete sequence through that consonant in my chain should not be presented as the demonstrated history of the ending. Shortening a long vowel before a cluster has a phonetic basis; the particular name history remains my analysis.

    My original proposed process

    Process: ✶mīri·wende > mīre·wende > mīre·wend > mīrwend > mirwend > mirwenn > mirwen

  • Alweth, AL-weth, “Wholesome One”, *alu “wholesome”, + -eth “feminine ending”

    Source status: Sindarin vocabulary with a Neo-Sindarin normalization. The name is presented here as my own creation.

    Tolkien wrote alw; *alu is the usual Neo-Sindarin rendering described by Eldamo. -eth is an attested feminine ending with a documented primitive ancestor. It is not a generic gender-neutral ending meaning “one”. The gloss above records my intended name meaning; the proposed full development is not an attested Tolkien derivation.

    My original proposed process

    Process: ✶alwa·itta > alwa·ittʰa > alwa·iθθa > alwa·eθθa > alwa·eθθ > alweθθ > alweθ

  • Tathreniel, tath-RE-ni-el, “Daughter of Willows”, tathren “of willow(s)”, + -iel¹ “daughter; feminine suffix”

    Source status: Sindarin adjective and name ending; a later formation. The name is presented here as my own creation.

    tathren is an adjective. -iel¹ can function as a daughter element or more generally as a feminine ending, so the kinship reading in my intended gloss is a choice, not something the ending guarantees. This short process joins already developed components. It illustrates a later formation, not a complete reconstruction from Primitive Elvish.

    My original proposed process

    Process: ✶tatʰren·iel > tathreniel

  • œ is a single historical vowel; oe is a diphthong. The symbol ø marks the absence of a sound.
  • The dagger (†) marks an earlier sound or sequence in this key. A macron marks vowel length; a breve explicitly marks shortness. Superscript ʰ marks aspiration and ʲ marks palatalization.
  • Phonetic j represents the consonantal sound often written i. Final written f can represent the sound [v].
  • In Eldamo’s patterns, V means a vowel, C a consonant, and S a syllable. Braces group alternatives, and a vertical bar separates alternatives or corresponding patterns. A leading or trailing hyphen indicates position; + or · marks a boundary.

The table follows the 78 entries in Paul Strack’s Sindarin phonetic index, checked on 14 September 2026. The columns show the rule, its example, and its number. The numbers follow Eldamo’s sequence; they are not instructions to apply every rule to every new compound.

The examples show selected stages, with the relevant change in bold. Click a rule title for the source discussion, or a [note] link for qualifications below the table. 1.6.4 Section Summary

  • A compound needs a supported meaning as well as a plausible sequence of sound changes.
  • Distinguish attested Sindarin elements, older vocabulary adopted for Neo-Sindarin, and the proposed history of a complete name.
  • A later formation and an ancient inherited compound need not have the same development.
  • Syllable position, stress, historical clusters, and recognized boundaries constrain which changes apply.
  • Use the linked rule discussions for exceptions. A short formula is a reference aid, not a universal substitution rule.
