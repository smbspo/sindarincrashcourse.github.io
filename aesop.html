i·Chebyr ah e·Vund

  • Back Translation: The Frogs & the Bull
  • Original: The Frogs & the Ox
Notes:
1 Reviewed on Vinyë Lambengolmor by Ellanto on 1/24/26.
§
Sindarin
Back Translation
Original
1.1
Mund odul n’ ael na lisg an hoged.
[A] Bull came to [a] pool of reeds to drink.
An Ox came down to a reedy pool to drink.
1.2
Lo falaechast vin nen vo dhram, nistant gabor ‘wain n’ e·vadha.
While he splashed into the water by (agent of) [a] heavy blow, he pressed [a] young frog into the mud.
As he splashed heavily into the water, he crushed a young Frog into the mud.
1.3
Rong e·gabor iaur anírant e·gabor bîn a mabent i·Chenair ah e·nethil dín man hídant anden.
Soon the old frog longed for the little frog and asked his brothers and sisters what happened to him.
The old Frog soon missed the little one and asked his brothers and sisters what had become of him.
1.4
“Úan mbelaith,” ebent ben na thin, “Abant bo chanar bîn vo vin i·thail velig dín!”
“[A] mighty monster,” said one of them, “he stepped on little brother with one of his great feet!”
"A great big monster," said one of them, "stepped on little brother with one of his huge feet!"
1.5
“Te vorg!” ebent e·gabor iaur, whestol1 en·ammos dín.
He [was] big! said the old frog, puffing her chest.
"Big, was he!" said the old Frog, puffing herself up.
1.6
“Borg ve hen?”
“Big like this?”
"Was he as big as this?"
1.7
“A! Ammorg!” agóner.
“O!, Bigger!” they shouted.
"Oh, much bigger!" they cried.
1.8
e·Gabor cwhestant1 en·ammos dín am.
The frog puffed her chest more.
The Frog puffed up still more.
1.9
“Ú-obul en·úan nad ammorg athar hen,” ebent.
“He could not be bigger beyond this,” she said.
"He could not have been bigger than this," she said.
1.10
Ach i·chebyr bîn il ebenner i ammorg en·úan ah e·gabor cwhestant1 en·ammos dín am ah am naden aranc.
But the little frogs all said that the monster was bigger and the old frog puffed his chest more and more until she burst.
But the little Frogs all declared that the monster was much, much bigger and the old Frog kept puffing herself out more and more until, all at once, she burst.
  • Pess: Avo ritho en·unadren.
  • Saying: Do not try the not-possible.
  • Motto: Do not attempt the impossible.
Notes:
1 whesta-, v. “to puff” ⪤ ᴹQ. hwesta-, v. “to puff”

Glingad nell bo ieath e·Vuig

  • Back Translation: Hanging [a] bell around [the] neck [of] the Cat
  • Original: Belling the Cat
Notes:
1 Updated 09/21/2026.
§
Sindarin
Back Translation
Original
1.1
Erlu i·nigyl govasser an amarthad banu al leithiad din ui goth dín, e·Vuig.
Once the mice gathered to decree [a] plan to free them from their enemy, the Cat.
The Mice once called a meeting to decide on a plan to free themselves of their enemy, the Cat.
1.2
Eno evírer hired leng1 istad io anglennast, to2 havathar aen lû nored ‘wa.
Still they wished to find a manner to have knowledge when she approached, so they would have time to run away.
At least they wished to find some way of knowing when she was coming, so they might have time to run away.
1.3
Î, boe andin hired leng1, an dorthasser vi thoss nan i·ñgemp dín to2 nabant ú-verthasser gwaed od i·chaew dín vin dû egor aur.
Indeed, [it] needs for them to find [a] way, for they dwelt in fear of her claws, so they almost did not dare to leave from their lairs in the night or day.
Indeed, something had to be done, for they lived in such constant fear of her claws that they hardly dared stir from their dens by night or day.
1.4
Athrabenner lae peny, ach ammódher i û vaer i·pheny hain.
They discussed a great number of plans, but they judged that those plans were not good.
Many plans were discussed, but none of them was thought good enough.
1.5
Na vedui Nigol dhae-‘wain eriast ah ebent:
At last [a] very young Mouse rose and said:
At last a very young Mouse got up and said:
1.6
“Sevin banu i nêf athgar, a bedhin i crannathab den.
“I have [a] plan that seems easy to do, and I judge that we accomplish it.
"I have a plan that seems very simple, but I know it will be successful.
1.7
Boe amben glingad nell bo iaeth e·Vuig.
[It] needs for us to hang [a] bell around [the] neck [of] the Cat.
All we have to do is to hang a bell about the Cat's neck.
1.8
Io lhassab e·nellad, to2 istab i e·goth vín dôl.”
When we hear the ringing bell, then we will have knowledge that our enemy comes.”
When we hear the bell ringing we will know immediately that our enemy is coming."
1.9
Gaedennin il i·Nigyl i allu óver nûth oh e·banu han.
Astounded [were] all the Mice that they never had [a] thought of this plan.
All the Mice were much surprised that they had not thought of such a plan before.
1.10
Ach lo ‘ellasser oh en·alvar dín, Nigol iaur eriast ah ebent:
But while they rejoiced about their good fortune, [an] old Mouse rose and said:
But in the midst of the rejoicing over their good fortune, an old Mouse arose and said:
1.11
“Pedathon i dhae vaer e·banu nan e·nigol ‘wain.
“I will say that very good [is] the plan of the young mouse.
"I will say that the plan of the young Mouse is very good.
1.12
Ach dhavo nin mabeded: Man glingatha e·nell bo iaeth e·Vuig?”
But allow me to ask [a] question: Who will hang the bell on [the] neck [of] the Cat?”
But let me ask one question: Who will bell the Cat?"
  • Pess: Te er nad peded i nad thô carnen, ach hith egel cared den.
  • Saying: It [is] one thing to say that [a] will be done, but another matter to do it.
  • Motto: It is one thing to say that something should be done, but quite a different matter to do it.
Notes:
1 leng, n. “way, method, manner” ⪤ Q. lé¹, n. “way, method, manner” [author: Rínor]
2 to, adv. “so, then” ⪤ Q. tá, adv. “then, [ᴹQ.] at that time [past]” [author: Elaran]

Nigol ui Garas ah Nigol ui Rídhas

  • Back Translation: [A] Mouse from the City and [A] Mouse from the Country
  • Original: The Town Mouse & the Country Mouse
Notes:
1 Updated 09/23/2026.
§
Sindarin
Back Translation
Original
1.1
Nigol ui Garas ‘lennast erlu cened ress i ndorthast vidh rídhas.
[A] Mouse from the City traveled to see [a] relative who lived in the country.
A Town Mouse once visited a relative who lived in the country.
1.2
An nedhwad e·Nigol ui Rídhas sestant thrimp na mbloss, sylch, nych, gu limig na nen ring an hoged cho1 den.
For lunch the Mouse from the Country set stalks of wheat, roots, and acorns, together with [a] small drop of cold water for drink before her.
For lunch the Country Mouse served wheat stalks, roots, and acorns, with a dash of cold water for drink.
1.3
e·Nigol ui Garas avant vi nenc dithin, nadhol níth1 na sen a níth1 na san, a mi leng dín tanant i avant vath thonnui2 air nad runnas3.
The Mouse from the City ate in tiny bites, chewing [a] piece of this and [a] piece of that, and in her manner, she indicated that she ate the basic food only to be polite.
The Town Mouse ate very sparingly, nibbling a little of this and a little of that, and by her manner making it very plain that she ate the simple food only to be polite.
1.4
Ab e·vâd i·mellyn ebenner anann, egos sennui e·Nigol ui Garas ebent oh e·guil dín viñ garas lo e·Nigol ui Rídhas lastast.
After the meal the friends spoke for long, or rather the Mouse from the City spoke about her life in the city while the Mouse from the Country listened.
After the meal the friends had a long talk, or rather the Town Mouse talked about her life in the city while the Country Mouse listened.
1.5
To4 gaedasser lored vi aedh laug viñ geilir5 ah olúrer vi dhîn a lhaugas6 naden amor.
Then they lay down to sleep in [a] cozy nest in the hedgerow and slept in silence and warmth until morning.
They then went to bed in a cozy nest in the hedgerow and slept in quiet and comfort until morning.
1.6
Lo lorol e·Nigol ui Rídhas olthant i de Nigol ui Garas i aw il e·vaeras a elvin na ndorthad viñ garas i e·vellon dín ebent anden.
While sleeping the Mouse from the Country dreamed that she [was a] Mouse from the City who had all the goodness and wonders of living in a city that her friend said to her.
In her sleep the Country Mouse dreamed she was a Town Mouse with all the luxuries and delights of city life that her friend had described for her.
1.7
To4 en·aur abor io e·Nigol ui Garas pichiant6 e·Nigol ui Rídhas glennad n’ e·mbâr dín viñ garas, te ‘lassui peded naw.
So the following day when the Mouse from the City asked for the Mouse from the Country to travel to her home in the city, she [was] happy to say yes.
So the next day when the Town Mouse asked the Country Mouse to go home with her to the city, she gladly said yes.
1.8
Io odúler n’ e·mbarthan ias7 e·Nigol ui Garas dorthast, egíner boe hardh vin ham am maded lemmath7 na mbereth elvain.
When they came to the mansion where the Mouse from the City dwelled, they saw on the table in the chamber for eating [were] leavings of a marvelous feast.
When they reached the mansion in which the Town Mouse lived, they found on the table in the dining room the leavings of a very fine banquet.
1.9
Ho din maith laich, pibingim9, besgyrn10 lîch, cuir gaudol, î, maith rothagol i Nigol obul núthad.
Before them [were] sweet foods and jams, sweet loaves, and tasteful cheeses, indeed, the most luring foods could conceive.
There were sweetmeats and jellies, pastries, delicious cheeses, indeed, the most tempting foods that a Mouse can imagine.
1.10
Ach io e·Nigol ui Rídhas fair maded níth1 bîn na mbasgorn10 laich, lhassant vuiad vrui na muig a rhibed n’ e·fend.
But when the Mouse from the Country [was] ready to eat [a] small piece of sweet loaf, she heard [a] loud meowing of [a] cat and scratching at the door.
But just as the Country Mouse was about to nibble a dainty bit of pastry, she heard a Cat mew loudly and scratch at the door.
1.11
Mi ‘oe i·Nigyl onúrer na had na thoblad, ias7 eríther úvenol an annas na lû, thossui thuiad.
In great fear the Mice ran to a place of hiding, where they remained unmoving for [a] length of time, fearful to breath.
In great fear the Mice scurried to a hiding place, where they lay quite still for a long time, hardly daring to breathe.
1.12
Io na vedui verthasser dammened n’ e·vereth, e·fend lediast vi rongas ah i·mbŷr vinnasser am buidad e·hardh, lo e·Chû aphant.
When at last they dared to go back to the feast, the door opened in haste and the servants entered to clean the table, while the Dog followed.
When at last they ventured back to the feast, the door opened suddenly and in came the servants to clear the table, followed by the House Dog.
1.13
e·Nigol ui Rídhas air bediast11 geded e·bôg ah e·rostobas12 dín viñ gaew nan e·Nigol ui Garas.
The Mouse of the Country only paused to get her bag and umbrella in the lair of the Mouse from the City.
The Country Mouse stopped in the Town Mouse's den only long enough to pick up her carpet bag and umbrella.
1.14
“No i hevig vaeras a math elvain i ú-hevin,” ebent lo rongast gwa, “ach vevin e·vath thonnui ah e·guithas thonnui nín midh Rídhas vi hîdh a beriad i thelir ‘u den.”
“Be that you have goodness and marvelous food that I do not have,” she said while she hastened away, “but I like my basic food and basic life in the country in peace and protection that comes together with it.”
You may have luxuries and dainties that I have not," she said as she hurried away, "but I prefer my plain food and simple life in the country with the peace and security that go with it."
  • Pess: Pem maelig ‘u veriad ammaer athar vaelig vig ‘oe a naid i ú-hinnin.
  • Saying: Without wealth together with protection [is] better beyond wealth among fear and things that [are] not known.
  • Motto: Poverty with security is better than plenty in the midst of fear and uncertainty.
Notes:
1 níth n. “piece, bit (of indeterminate size)” ⪤ ᴺQ. !nihta
2 thonnui, adj. “basic” from thond, n. “root, [N.] base; ⚠️root-word”
3 runnas, v. “polite, politeness” from ᴺQ. !rundië n. “smoothness; politeness”
4 to, adv. “so, then” ⪤ Q. tá, adv. “then, [ᴹQ.] at that time [past]” [author: Elaran]
5 ceilir, n. “hedgerow” from S. cai n. “hedge” + S. #lîr n. “line, [N.] row” [author: Elaran]
6 pichia- “request, ask for” kwisja- > pisja- > pixja- > pexja- > pexia- > S pe(i)chia- with optional i-intrusion; pechia- [author: Röandil]
7 ias, conj. “where (relative)” ⪤ Q. yassë, conj. “where [relative], in which place” [author: Unknown]
8 lemmath, n. “left over food, leavings” from ᴹ✶lebnā “left behind” + ᴺS. [ᴱN.] math n. “Food”
